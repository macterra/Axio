# Volume 3 continuity and copyedit

Date: 2026-07-29

Scope: continuous reading of the Volume 3 introduction and all 36 chapters, with particular attention to the boundaries among functional modeling, consciousness, sentience, agency, current artificial systems, legal claims, and the handoff to the Volume 4 alignment program.

## Continuity result

The volume's governing taxonomy holds. Modeling, cognition, consciousness, sentience, sapience, suffering, and agency remain separate evidentiary claims. The consciousness sequence presents functional architectures and testable correlates while leaving phenomenality as a controversial identity premise. The Agency Criterion evaluates the whole deployed system rather than inferring agency from fluent model output. The risk and governance sequence keeps its conclusions conditional on empirical and normative premises.

Four residual problems required repair. Chapter 1 described artificial systems as partner-minds that hold the whole philosophy in view, contrary to the later human-verification discipline. Chapter 6 stated a speculative projection account as settled cognitive geometry immediately before Chapter 7 introduced a candidate architecture. Chapter 12 omitted changing the adverse condition from its list of interventions on suffering. Chapter 19 treated a contested copyright question as categorically resolved and denied memorization or reproduction by definition.

## Applied repairs

- **Chapter 1:** the Dialectic Catalyst becomes a structured human–AI workflow in which artificial systems assist criticism and coherence while the human retains verification and whole-system responsibility.
- **Chapter 6:** the projection account remains vivid but is consistently marked as a functional hypothesis compatible with, rather than demonstrative of, the Modeler-Schema account.
- **Chapter 12:** the proposal now exposes three levers on represented-versus-preferred divergence: change the condition, correct the representation, or adjust the preference.
- **Chapter 19:** learned generalization is separated from mere retrieval without turning that functional distinction into a legal verdict. The text now acknowledges copying during dataset preparation, memorization, substantially similar outputs, market substitution, and case-specific fair-use analysis, anchored to the U.S. Copyright Office's May 2025 report.
