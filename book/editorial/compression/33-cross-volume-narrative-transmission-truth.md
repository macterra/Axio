# Cross-Volume Audit: Narrative Transmission and Truth

## Scope

This audit tests the map-and-territory bridge between:

- Volume 2 Chapter 6, which owns model mediation and domain-relative adequacy;
- Volume 8 Chapter 1, which introduces narrative transmission and legend formation; and
- later applications in Volumes 8 and 9.

## Ownership rule

Every empirical account is model-mediated, but models and stories remain answerable to their targets. Cultural transmission can reward accuracy, resonance, coordination, prestige, repetition, authority, or practical success in different combinations.

Fitness therefore cannot be shortened to “fit to mind rather than fit to world.” To claim selection for a narrative trait requires identified variants, carriers, transmission or retention, differential outcomes, an environment, and a horizon. A plausible adaptive function is not by itself a reconstructed selection history.

## Findings

### Volume 2 Chapter 6: retain

The canonical chapter distinguishes purposeful omission from error, keeps the territory independent of representation, and ranks maps by performance within a stated domain. Its later links name new failures rather than re-running the epistemology.

### Volume 8 Chapter 1: revise

The chapter's central section already states the correct conditional mechanism: accuracy is one fitness factor among several, weak in some environments and decisive in others; resonance dominates only where operative filters reward it more reliably.

Four surrounding passages overrode that guard:

- the opening called one evolutionary account simply true;
- adaptive functions were treated as proving that narrative was “selected and selected hard”;
- three historical examples were said to settle the causal claim that usefulness rather than accuracy drives legend;
- the conclusion called culture a layer selected not for world-fit but mind-fit.

The revision keeps the story-telling-ape thesis and every example while distinguishing a plausible selection hypothesis from demonstrated history, treating the three legends as illustrations of a mechanism, and making cognitive fit compete with rather than replace world fit.

### Later bridges: retain

Volume 9 Chapter 5 uses an altered-map link to separate phenomenology from metaphysics. Volume 9 Chapter 18 uses map failure to distinguish falsehood from thermodynamic entropy. Volume 8's later chapters specify additional transmission, capture, or enforcement mechanisms. None repeats the canonical map argument.

## Decision

Retain the map/territory motif and the narrative-fitness bridge. Narrative transmission need not track accuracy, but neither biological nor cultural selection is indifferent to truth by definition.
