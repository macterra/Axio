# Phase 7 Cross-Volume No-Value-Without-a-Valuer Audit

Baseline: July 26, 2026, after the faith/update-rule audit.

## Scope

This audit tests the repetition-ledger instruction to let later chapters inherit the canonical case against valuer-independent value without collapsing three different relations:

- **Agent-relative value** orders possibilities for a valuer under a comparison and context.
- **Market valuation** expresses or infers parts of those orderings through bids, prices, sacrifice, and opportunity cost under specified institutions and constraints.
- **Existential meaning** is enacted and sustained through agents, practices, relationships, interpretation, and participation; it is neither an intrinsic property nor a market price.

Volume 5 Chapter 1 owns the canonical thesis: valuing is an activity of agents, practical reasons acquire force within forms of life, and removing every possible valuer removes the standpoint from which better and worse can matter. The chapter presents this as a substantive relational axiom and argument, not a semantic victory over every position called moral realism.

The audit followed links to the canonical chapter, searched all manuscript volumes for objective, subjective, intrinsic, agent-relative, and valuer-relative value language, and read the principal Volume 5, Volume 6, and Volume 9 sequences continuously. The ownership test asked:

1. Does a later passage repeat Chapter 1's objects, public-goods case, or foundation arguments?
2. Does it instead bind a moral claim, interpret behavioral evidence, define a market quantity, or construct existential meaning?
3. Does a volume need one compact bridge to remain independently readable?
4. Does the wording preserve the valuer, comparison, conditions, and relevant kind of value?

## Canonical ownership

Volume 5 Chapter 1 remains intact. It owns the rainforest, artwork, theorem, and public-goods cases; the moral corollary; the God, reason, evolution, and intuition candidates; and the answer to nihilism. The glossary retains the compact reader-facing definition and directs readers to both the canonical chapter and the evidence account in Chapter 4.

## Compressed recital

Volume 5 Chapter 4 repeated Chapter 1's public-goods prosecution and then previewed the market volume's analysis of funding mechanisms. The revised section keeps the point Chapter 4 uniquely needs: costly, informed, voluntary support is evidence, but nonpayment is ambiguous under non-excludability, strategic withholding, constrained means, or a poor mechanism. It now inherits the case against valuer-independent worth and hands the mechanism analysis to Volume 6 Chapters 4 and 8.

**Decision:** Compress the repeated public-goods case; preserve the free-rider qualification on revealed preference.

## Corrected relation in the meaning volume

Volume 9 Chapter 14 described “value” itself as a structural dynamic through which coherence propagates. That wording could make value sound like an agent-independent process after the chapter had correctly denied a cosmic scoreboard. The revised formulation names **valuing** as an agential dynamic: agents select and propagate coherence they value. The chapter's Meaningness synthesis and its claim about participation remain intact.

**Decision:** Restore the valuer to the relation without cutting the existential synthesis.

## Retained applications

### Ethics and normative application

Volume 5 Chapters 2–3 extend the canonical thesis to agent-binding and norms; Chapter 4 owns sacrifice as defeasible evidence; Chapters 5–7 rebuild ethical traditions, chosen commitments, and judgment under explicit premises. Chapters 14 and 16 test aggregation and rival realist proposals. Chapter 18 distinguishes persistence from value, while Chapter 26 closes the promised loop by recovering public assessability under an avowed normative premise.

These chapters answer what follows after the canonical thesis rather than arguing for it again. Their compact opening bridges are necessary to the volume's relay.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Markets, prices, and attention

Volume 6 Chapter 1 restates the relational axiom once for an independently readable applied volume, then adds tradeoffs, numeraires, and limits on scalar representation. Chapters 2–4 distinguish value, wealth, capital, money, currency, and price, culminating in the funding test and its qualifications. Chapter 5 applies opportunity cost to attention while distinguishing salience, relevance, and reflective value. Later market chapters use the same premise to analyze incentives, purchasing power, coordination, and AGI preferences.

Prices are public facts about transactions under conditions; they are neither intrinsic worth nor complete aggregations of affected agents' values. Removing the bridges would blur rather than reduce that distinction.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Governance and culture

The Volume 7 introduction and Chapters 1, 22, and 35 use agent-relative value to delimit individual standing, utopian claims, and price-informed governance. Volume 8 Chapter 4 expressly prevents the Axionic Criterion from becoming a smuggled objective value: reciprocal agency preservation is an adopted commitment, not a ranking issued by the cosmos.

These are jurisdictional safeguards, not recitals.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Existential meaning

Volume 9 Chapter 2 studies the protected role of sacred commitments. Chapter 7 tests the Stoic claim that virtue is objectively good. Chapter 10 declares a first-person credo; Chapter 11 locates the end of practical justification in terminal valuing; Chapter 12 separates interpreted truth, agent-relative value, and enacted meaning; Chapter 13 treats reflective endorsement and inheritance; and Chapter 14 synthesizes participation with agency.

These passages explain how meaning can be real without being valuer-independent. Market price, moral authority, protected commitment, terminal value, and existential significance remain different relations.

**Decision:** Retain, with the Chapter 14 terminology correction above.
