Baseline: July 28, 2026, after the attractors, lenses, and filters audit.

## Result

Volume 4 Chapter 4 retains the Sovereign Kernel as a specific proposed architecture at three depths: constitutive boundary, partial evaluation, and deterministic execution. Later uses of *kernel* for a minimal semantic or historical core are ordinary lexical uses; operating-system language remains analogical unless a chapter supplies actual process isolation, capability control, execution, or adjudication machinery.

Volume 5 Chapter 23 previously called its Procedural Layer an epistemic operating system that “determines” whether a boundary violation occurred, then concluded that ethics is an operating system that runs on real hardware. Those formulations overstated both the metaphor and the procedure. The chapter itself makes adjudication fallible, distributed where possible, evidence-bound, appealable, and dependent on legitimate authority.

The revision names the layer a fallible adjudication process at first use. Its conclusion now limits the operating-system analogy to the need for rules that can handle ambiguity, error, conflict, and divergent perception. The invariant states a proposed boundary; procedure applies it without becoming an infallible truth machine or acquiring authority merely by existing.

The remaining Volume 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, and 8 uses preserve their local jobs: semantic core, civic infrastructure, ideological framework, actual computer software, capability isolation, or historical core. None competes with the Sovereign Kernel's formal ownership.
