# Phase 7 Cross-Volume Viability/Persistence Audit

Baseline: July 27, 2026, after the sacrifice/opportunity-cost audit.

## Scope

This audit tests the repetition-ledger instruction to keep descriptive persistence, indexed viability, strategic equilibrium, and the chosen protection of agency separate.

The audit searched the complete manuscript for the viability, persistence, survival, and survivorship families. It then read the canonical Volume 5 Chapters 18–22 sequence continuously and reviewed the concentrated later applications in Volume 1 Chapters 23–27, Volume 4 Chapters 3–6 and 10, Volume 7 Chapter 34, Volume 8 Chapters 3–4, and Volume 9 Chapters 14 and 18.

The ownership test asked:

1. What is the unit: organism, agent, preference architecture, institution, cultural pattern, lineage, or represented event sector?
2. What function or identity is preserved, under which conditions and over what horizon?
3. Does persistence name a descriptive outcome, a mechanism, or a value chosen by an agent?
4. Does the passage infer truth, usefulness, agency, merit, legitimacy, or moral authority from survival alone?

## Canonical ownership

Volume 5 Chapter 18 owns the ultimate-metagame lens and its limits. Persistence is a precondition for the continued action of a specified agent, commitment, or successor pattern, not a universal objective function or one scoreboard. Selection describes survivorship without supplying a complete explanation of whatever survives.

Chapter 19 owns the indexed definition. A viability claim identifies the unit, preserved function or identity, environment and constraints, perturbations, horizon, and failure baseline. Persistence of one unit does not establish the health, agency, or legitimacy of another.

Chapters 20–22 move from prospective loss and responsibility to an avowed normative premise. They do not derive reciprocal agency protection from persistence. Coercive orders, deception, lock-in, and exploitation can remain viable under some conditions; the Ethics of Viability chooses which capacities and relationships to protect.

## Corrected cultural inference

Volume 8 Chapter 3 already supplies the correct descriptive account: cultural fitness is differential propagation under specified variants, filters, environments, populations, and horizons, not intrinsic merit or what agents value. Chapter 4 later slipped across that boundary in five linked passages.

The revision preserves the river metaphor, exemplar mechanism, distributed-instantiation account, and Axionic Criterion while correcting the inference:

- Centuries of continuity establish durable transmission, not that carriers freely judged a pattern worth its cost.
- Usefulness and agency support are possible causes of continuation alongside inheritance, institutions, coercion, dependency, path dependence, and chance.
- Exemplars attract as exemplars, while institutions built around them may command or enforce.
- Cultural variants propagate better under operative filters; they are not thereby “superior.”
- Passing or failing the chosen Axionic Criterion can affect voluntary propagation without mechanically determining cultural survival.

The last point keeps the chapter's descriptive and normative halves distinct. Selection will not enforce the Criterion; agents and accountable institutions must apply the chosen standard.

**Decision:** Correct the category drift; retain the chapter's structure and voice.

## Retained applications

### Physical identity and branching

Volume 1 uses persistence for self-maintenance, identity across transformation, observer continuity, and represented future continuations. The chapters state their relevant pattern, mapping, constraints, event sector, horizon, coarse-graining, and identity criterion. The Quantum Metagame explicitly denies that Measure is value or that a pattern owns an amount of future.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Reflective and engineered viability

Volume 4 distinguishes reflective stability from durability under selection and tests specified agent architectures under declared pressures. Chapter 10 separates coherence from viability and records collapse, stasis, and growth regimes without treating survival as alignment or benevolence.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Institutional admissibility

Volume 7 Chapter 34 evaluates governance systems through command, ownership, beneficiaries, contestability, exit, and dependence. Institutional persistence is not its legitimacy test, and ordinary relocation cost is not confused with coercive lock-in.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Meaning and meta-myth

Volume 9 Chapter 14 uses persistence as indexed pattern invariance while preserving the gap between system dynamics and ethical verdict. Chapter 18 states the controlling limit directly: cancers, tyrannies, and falsehoods can persist; the Law of Coherence is a chosen orientation rather than authority read from survival.

**Decision:** Retain.
