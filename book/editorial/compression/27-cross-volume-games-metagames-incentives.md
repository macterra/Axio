# Phase 7 Games, Metagames, and Incentives Audit

Baseline: July 28, 2026, after the harm, coercion, consent, force, and violence audit.

## Result

Volume 5 Chapter 18 retains the canonical metagame lens. An ordinary metagame requires specified players, goals or payoffs, rules, levels, and a channel through which higher-level incentives change lower-level strategy. The “ultimate metagame” is more limited: an analytic model of differential persistence among declared units under specified mechanisms and horizons, not a literal universal game, chooser, objective function, or complete explanation of survival.

Volume 6 Chapter 15 correctly owns the institutional transmission mechanism but allowed four recaps to outrun that ceiling. It said incentives propagate through every lower level, treated misalignment as morally exculpatory, described institutional preservation as a system optimizing through a persistence metagame, and explained system collapse through incentives generally.

The revision restores the boundaries:

- cross-level propagation requires an evidenced causal channel and can remain partial;
- a structural incentive diagnosis does not erase attributable deception, negligence, or abuse;
- continued funding, staffing, and authority can select for institutional preservation without creating an institution-level chooser;
- incentive-driven collapse is one mechanism alongside shocks, incompetence, conflict, resource loss, and causes outside the taxonomy.

The remaining Volume 1, 5, 6, and 8 applications preserve distinct physical, viability, institutional, and cultural work. They specify whether the relevant units are agents, strategies, institutions, or patterns; keep persistence descriptive; and do not infer truth, value, agency, or universal optimization from survival.
