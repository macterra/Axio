# Phase 7 Harm, Coercion, Consent, Force, and Violence Recap Audit

Baseline: July 28, 2026, after the Measure and Credence audit.

## Result

Volume 5 Chapters 9–12 retain the operational taxonomy: harm is a material setback relative to an explicit and appropriate baseline; coercion is a credible conditional threat used deliberately to obtain compliance; consent is scoped, intentional, informed, capacity-grounded, and voluntary authorization; force directly compels; and violence is injurious or destructive physical force. Chapter 20 extends harm prospectively to material, foreseeable, attributable exposure increases without making risk, wrongfulness, or coercion synonyms.

Five later recaps blurred those boundaries. The revisions:

- restore welfare, capacity, viable options, and baseline analysis to a Volume 6 harm recap rather than treating agency loss or threat as the only routes to harm;
- require a credible compliance-shaped threat before private influence or suppression is called coercion in Volume 7;
- describe operational speech as participation in violence rather than saying words become physical force;
- separate descriptive harm from wrongfulness in Volume 8's disassociation analysis;
- replace claims that plans inevitably become coercive, or that reality can consent, with an agent-bound mechanism and conditional institutional risk;
- distinguish coercion from force and from manipulation by asymmetric framing in Volume 9.

Consent required no independent line edit. The audited applications preserve capacity, understanding, voluntariness, scope, and prospective revocation; none treated a signature, hard choice, residence, or aggregate participation as sufficient authorization.

The family is now inherited without turning harm into wrongfulness, influence into coercion, lack of consent into harm by definition, force into violence, or violent operational participation into a property of words alone.

## Protected-material ledger

- Preserve Volume 5 Chapters 9–12 and 20 as the canonical treatments.
- Preserve Volume 7 Chapter 9's expression, preparatory-propaganda, and delegated-violence ladder; operational participation can be executed through speech without redefining violence as speech.
- Preserve applied chapters' examples, verdicts, and argumentative ownership.
- Do not infer wrongfulness from impact, consent from compliance, coercion from influence or asymmetry alone, or legitimacy from classification.
