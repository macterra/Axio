# Phase 7 Cross-Volume Faith/Update-Rule Audit

Baseline: July 26, 2026, after the purpose-relative-model audit.

## Scope

This audit tests the repetition-ledger instruction to bridge later uses of faith to the canonical argument without repeating its prosecution. Volume 2 Chapter 10 owns the stipulated target: **epistemic faith** is a belief protected from relevant correction by a disabled or frozen update rule. The chapter expressly excludes trust, loyalty, hope, religious practice, practical resolve, and other commitments unless they also immunize a claim.

The audit followed direct links to the canonical chapter, searched the manuscript for faith, calibration, immunity, frozen-update, and error-correction language, and read the Volume 2 and Volume 9 candidate sequences continuously. The ownership test asked:

1. Does a later passage repeat Chapter 10's definition, pilot example, or case against treating reason itself as faith?
2. Does it instead apply corrigibility to a new belief, institution, practice, or experience?
3. Is *faith* being used in the stipulated epistemic sense, in an explicitly broader religious sense, or in an unrelated phrase such as *bad faith* or *good faith*?
4. Would compression erase a locally necessary distinction among belief, trust, fidelity, revelation, inherited commitment, and resolve?

## Canonical ownership

Volume 2 Chapter 10 remains intact. It owns the fog-pilot contrast, the definition of a frozen update rule, the pragmatic, epistemic, moral, religious, existential, and aesthetic cases, and the final distinction between corrigible conviction and epistemic immunity. The reader-facing glossary retains the compact lookup definition and the same exclusions.

## Compressed recitals and corrected terminology

Three passages in the surrounding Volume 2 sequence required changes:

- Chapter 16 described an explicitly rejectable, actively disputed epistemic posit as a principle taken “on faith.” That wording conflicted with Chapter 10's stipulated definition. The passage now calls it an **epistemic premise** and preserves every qualification and the live rival.
- Chapter 17 repeated Chapter 10's fog-pilot example and confidence/immunity explanation while answering the paralysis objection to pancritical rationalism. It now inherits the distinction by link and keeps its local claim: fallibility does not prevent action.
- Chapter 18 re-ran Chapter 10's argument that an upstream assumption is not thereby faith while answering whether coherence rests on arbitrary axioms. It now inherits that distinction and preserves the chapter-specific work: paraconsistent and dialetheic reasoners can reject universal non-contradiction while retaining discriminating constraints, and pancritical criticism leaves the canons themselves open.

**Decision:** Correct one misuse and compress two inherited defenses.

## Retained applications

### Conditionalist sequence

Volume 2 Chapter 8 anticipates the canonical definition while deriving faith as a failure of belief calibration. Chapter 18's earlier scope note distinguishes some immunized faith commitments from religious reasoners generally. Chapter 19 makes permanent refusal of stable negative feedback the terminal limit of its updating practice. Chapter 24 distinguishes updating within a model from criticism and reconstruction between models. Chapter 25 applies immunization to coalition-protected frames, and the volume introduction supplies orientation.

Each passage adds a different epistemic operation or application rather than replaying Chapter 10's case law.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Ethics, markets, and culture

Volume 5 Chapter 8 treats lying as an attack on another agent's calibration. Volume 6 Chapter 14 contrasts frozen confidence with Bitcoin's repeated adversarial verification. Volume 8 Chapter 20 applies error-correction failure to gated communities and self-curated epistemic enclosure; Chapter 6's event horizon describes a related frame pathology without redefining faith.

These are local causal applications. The Bitcoin passage needs the compact definition because the chapter's own religious metaphor makes the contrast explicit.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Meaning and religion

The opening Volume 9 sequence does not rebuild the prosecution of faith. Chapter 1 explicitly adopts the canonical judgment and turns to the experiences that survive a naturalistic reinterpretation. Chapters 2–5 then distinguish protected practical commitments, inherited sacred narratives, naturalistic deflations, and revelation claims that require independent evidence. Their work is constructive and evidential, not another definition of epistemic faith.

Later passages preserve the same boundary. Chapter 13 refuses to equate religious belief with immaturity and locates maturity in reflective authorship. Chapter 15 distinguishes trust and fidelity from its deliberately narrow use of Faith as a designed “sin.” Chapter 19 asks successors to test an inherited request instead of obeying its source. Chapter 20's final letter uses fidelity, trust, and religious faith in their broader senses while restating only the narrow requirement that metaphysical conclusions remain answerable to evidence. Its reconciliatory voice depends on those distinctions.

**Decision:** Retain.

Ordinary phrases such as *good faith*, *bad faith*, *leap of faith*, and *faithful execution* remain untouched where context makes clear that they do not invoke the stipulated epistemic term.
