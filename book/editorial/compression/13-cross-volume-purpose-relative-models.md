# Phase 7 Cross-Volume Purpose-Relative Model Audit

Baseline: July 26, 2026, after the Conditionalism recap audit.

## Scope

This audit tests the repetition-ledger instruction to compress routine restatements of purpose-relative modeling while retaining passages that introduce a new model layer. The canonical treatment is divided deliberately:

- Volume 2 Chapter 6 owns the epistemology: empirical knowledge is model-mediated, models preserve and omit structure, and model choice is assessed within a domain.
- Volume 3 Chapter 2 owns the anatomy: a model is a purpose-relative structured representation, explicit or implicit, whose adequacy depends on the task.

The audit followed direct links to both chapters, searched for the associated purpose, task, domain, adequacy, preservation, omission, and map–territory language, and read the candidate sequences continuously. The ownership test asked:

1. Does the later passage merely restate the canonical definition or example?
2. Does it introduce a new modeled object, representational layer, task, or failure mode?
3. Does an independently readable volume need a compact bridge?
4. Can the later passage inherit the definition without losing a premise required by its local argument?

## Canonical ownership

The two canonical chapters remain intact. Their shared Underground-map example gives Volume 3 an independently readable entry point, but they answer different questions. Volume 2 explains why knowledge cannot step outside representation; Volume 3 specifies what qualifies as a model and separates explicit, implicit, generative, and attributed models. Neither chapter can inherit the other's argument wholesale.

The reader-facing glossary also retains its compact definition and links to both canonical treatments.

## Retained applications

### Conditionalist epistemology

Volume 2 Chapters 1 and 3 locate scientific and correspondence claims inside models. Chapters 7–9 then use preserved invariants, models of agents, and domain-bounded reliability to build realism, belief, and knowledge. Chapter 14 invokes explanatory structure in the Measure/Credence problem; Chapter 22 applies purpose-relative carving to probability event-spaces; and Chapter 25 includes model mediation in the volume's final synthesis.

These passages inherit the canonical account but add a new epistemic object or test. Chapter 22's return to the map example is locally earned: it shows exactly what a probability event-space preserves and discards rather than illustrating models in general.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Minds and machines

The Volume 3 introduction and Chapter 1 orient the reader toward the canonical model anatomy. Chapters 4–10 introduce successive layers—deliberative models, reflective self-models, semantic interpretation, inner-speech geometry, the Modeler-Schema architecture, and the Agency-Model proposal. Chapters 20–23 test causal models, fluency, behavioral evidence, and deployed-system agency. In each case the model layer is the chapter's subject rather than a recap of the general definition.

Chapter 3 and Chapter 19 contained the two exceptions:

- Chapter 3 repeated the immediately preceding implicit-model primer and formal definition before applying them to regulation. The revision now inherits the implicit-model account by link, keeps one control-specific example, and states how task, relevant structure, and purpose map onto regulation.
- Chapter 19 repeated both the canonical definition and the Underground-map example before reaching its vector case. The revision inherits the definition in one sentence and lets the fresh music example carry the application.

**Decision:** Compress the two inherited primers; retain the new model layers.

### Physics, alignment, and later applications

Volume 1 Chapter 6 uses stale maps to explain the loss of control under isolation. Chapter 8 defines the QBU as a purpose-specific modeling layer, and Chapter 16 makes triviality a model-relative judgment under a declared purpose and horizon. Volume 4 Chapters 2–3 distinguish systems that can be aligned from forces that can only be modeled and show why goals apply to model-generated descriptions rather than reality without interpretation.

Volume 5 Chapter 18 delimits game theory as an analytic lens with specified players, payoffs, and selection mechanisms. Volume 8 Chapter 1 adds culture as a second distortion layer selected for resonance, while Chapter 19 distinguishes the authority of physical modeling from the social choice of purposes. Volume 9 Chapter 5 uses an altered map to separate psychedelic phenomenology from metaphysical inference; Chapter 14 makes existential meaning a relation among a modeling agent, its purposes, and a constraining world.

Each passage supplies a domain-specific consequence rather than another model definition.

**Decision:** Retain.
