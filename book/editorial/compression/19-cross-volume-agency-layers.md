# Phase 7 Cross-Volume Agency-Layer Audit

Baseline: July 27, 2026, after the QBU-primer audit.

## Scope

This audit tests the repetition-ledger instruction to preserve one basal definition while distinguishing reflective authorship, sovereignty, standing, effective agency, institutional agency, political sovereignty, and scoped authority.

The audit searched the complete manuscript for definition-bearing uses of agency, authorship, sovereignty, the Agency Criterion, effective agency, and institutional agency. It read the canonical Volume 1 Chapters 2 and 5 and Volume 4 Chapters 2 and 4, then reviewed the concentrated applications in Volume 3 Chapter 23, Volume 5 Chapters 13 and 26, Volume 6 Chapter 15, Volume 7 Chapters 2, 30, and 33, Volume 8 Chapters 3–4, Volume 9 Chapter 6, and all volume introductions.

The ownership test asked:

1. Is the passage describing causal capacity, basal model–evaluation–control, reflective authorship, or sovereignty?
2. Does it make consciousness, moral standing, legitimacy, wisdom, or benevolence follow from a weaker layer?
3. Does effective or institutional agency replace the underlying control-loop definition rather than qualify its exercise or scale?
4. Is a later passage re-deriving the hierarchy, or does its compact bridge perform necessary local work?

## Canonical ownership

Volume 1 Chapter 2 owns basal agency: an embedded system models reachable futures, evaluates them internally, and makes behavior depend on that evaluation so as to steer outcomes relative to a baseline. Chapter 5 supplies the minimum cases and boundary tests.

Volume 4 Chapter 2 owns reflective authorship: temporal continuity, reason ownership, commitment revision, and authored transition. Chapter 4 owns sovereignty: the evaluative machinery remains causally authoritative and can stop an otherwise feasible action. Neither layer guarantees benevolence or correct judgment.

The remaining qualified uses keep their established homes:

- Volume 3 Chapter 23 owns the deployed-system Agency Criterion.
- Volume 5 Chapters 13 and 26 own sovereign standing and its normative premise.
- Volume 7 Chapter 2 owns effective political agency.
- Volume 7 Chapter 33 owns attributable institutional authorship.
- Political sovereignty remains a coercive jurisdictional claim rather than reflective sovereignty.

## Corrected construction claim

Volume 3 Chapter 8 correctly denied an uncaused homunculus but then called agency itself a “pragmatic reality” and an “indispensable construct,” grouping the self with money, borders, and laws. That move shifted from the narrated self being model-constructed to the basal control process being merely socially or pragmatically real.

The revision preserves the chapter's anti-essentialist argument while restoring the canonical hierarchy:

- The narrator can be constructed without agency being illusory.
- Agency belongs to the physically implemented model–evaluation–control process.
- The narrated self is a higher-level model within that process, not a separate cause.
- Reflective authorship, rather than every control loop, is the layer to which this book attaches its chosen ethical standing.
- Denying an uncaused inner essence leaves basal control and the further authorship question intact.

**Decision:** Correct the layer substitution; retain the chapter's structure and Dennett engagement.

## Retained bridges

### Machine agency

Volume 3 Chapter 23 does not repeat the basal definition. It converts the earlier criteria into four intervention families for a whole deployed system: persistence, preference integrity, counterfactual ownership, and consequence-bearing control. The stricter evidence profile tests ownership and continuity in composite systems rather than redefining bacteria out of basal agency.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Standing and ethics

Volume 5 applies reflective and sovereign qualifiers where moral standing is at issue and keeps sentient welfare on a separate axis. The passages disclose reciprocal protection as a normative commitment rather than a physical consequence of basal control.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Markets, politics, and institutions

Volume 6 uses reflective authorship for choosing which incentive gradients to inhabit. Volume 7 uses effective agency for the exercise of existing capacities under political conditions and institutional agency only where decision, responsibility, revision, and endorsement remain attributable. Political sovereignty names claimed coercive supremacy, not a group mind.

**Decision:** Retain.

### Culture and meaning

Volume 8 leaves interpretation and responsibility with people unless an organization independently meets the Agency Criterion. Volume 9 Chapter 6 gives one compact basal-versus-reflective reminder before adding wisdom; later uses concern endorsement, participation, and self-authorship rather than another definition.

**Decision:** Retain.
