# Phase 7 Cross-Volume Objective Morality and Conditional Realism Audit

Baseline: July 27, 2026, after the consciousness, sentience, and sapience audit.

## Result

Volume 5 Chapters 1–3 retain the substantive relational thesis that value requires a valuer, the separation of attribution from application, and the distinction between causal constraint and normative obligation. Chapter 26 retains the limited reconstruction: conditional objectivity means public assessability under disclosed inputs, while Sapient Agency Realism adds a chosen reciprocal-authorship premise rather than deriving an ought from agency structure.

Volume 5 Chapter 22 previously called value without a valuer a “category error” after Chapter 1 had explicitly presented agent-relative value as a substantive axiom rather than a definitional victory. The same recap said moral claims bind only the agents whose values give them content, collapsing adoption into coverage before Chapter 26 distinguishes them.

The revision restores both firewalls:

- Agent-relative value is the volume's defended premise, not a conclusion obtained merely by labeling its rival ill-typed.
- A framework can conditionally classify conduct within its declared domain even when the target rejects its premise.
- Valid application does not establish agent-independent authority, motivation, enforcement legitimacy, or universal adoption.
- Facts about coherence, viability, suffering, and agency constrain application but supply no terminal normative premise.

The remaining recaps in Volume 2 Chapter 2 and Volume 5 Chapters 5–7, 14, 16, 18–21, and 27 perform distinct local work and preserve the separation among premise, attribution, application, authority, and enforcement.
