# Phase 7 Cross-Volume Sacredness, Meaning, and Chosen Commitments Audit

Baseline: July 27, 2026, after the objective-morality and conditional-realism audit.

## Result

Volume 3 Chapter 5 retains semantic meaning as interpreted aboutness. Volume 9 Chapter 2 retains sacredness as a protected role rather than proof that its occupant is true or good. Chapters 10–14 retain existential meaning and reflective commitment, while Chapter 18 retains the meta-myth as an avowed narrative orientation whose empirical spine remains answerable to evidence.

Two late Volume 9 recaps blurred those jurisdictions:

- Chapter 14 said meaning was “made” in the transition from chaos to coherence after defining it as a relation among agents, purposes, participation, and a constraining world. The revision keeps coherence as part of the process without treating physical or dynamical order as sufficient for existential meaning.
- Chapter 15 said meaning itself could be engineered immediately after denying that a designed system can manufacture significance on demand. The revision distinguishes designed supporting practices from the meaning agents may enact through participation, interpretation, endorsement, revision, or refusal.

The remaining recaps preserve the governing firewalls:

- Semantic meaning, causal function, represented purpose, and existential significance remain distinct.
- Chosen commitments may be caused or inherited; choice names reflective endorsement and answerability.
- Sacredness identifies protected priority before evaluating its occupant.
- Terminality ends an instrumental chain without proving a universal apex or immunity from reflection.
- The Great Unfolding remains a meta-myth, not physical teleology or a normative theorem.
- Provenance supports interpretation without creating unchanged identity or successor debt.
