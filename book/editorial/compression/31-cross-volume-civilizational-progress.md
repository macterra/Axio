# Cross-Volume Audit: Civilizational Progress

## Scope

This audit tests whether *progress*, *evolution*, and *civilization* retain separate jobs across the late volumes:

- Volume 6 Chapter 19 measures progress against declared human outcomes and names the mechanisms that produced them.
- Volume 8 Chapters 3 and 6 treat cultural evolution as variation, transmission or retention, and differential outcomes under local environments and horizons; selection supplies no global direction called improvement.
- Volume 7 Chapters 28 and 32 turn those descriptive materials into a political program.

## Ownership rule

Differential persistence can explain why an institutional form spreads or survives. It cannot by itself establish that the form is an improvement, that history has one objective, or that agency and reduced coercion are civilization's evolved direction.

Progress therefore requires:

1. a stated evaluator or standard;
2. a specified outcome and baseline;
3. a causal account of the change; and
4. room for reversals, tradeoffs, and rival criteria.

The book may choose agency preservation and reduced coercion as political standards. It may not read that choice out of cultural selection or the direction of history.

## Findings

### Volume 6 Chapter 19: retain

The chapter's child-mortality, life-expectancy, sanitation, energy, information, and capital claims are indexed to recognizable human outcomes. Its forecast is dated, probabilistic, and explicitly vulnerable to reversal. The word *progress* is evaluative there, but the evaluator and evidence are supplied.

### Volume 8 Chapters 3 and 6: retain

These chapters own the technical boundary. Cultural fitness is local and descriptive; no global improvement follows from persistence. Their agency criterion is declared as a chosen normative standard rather than an evolutionary destination.

### Volume 7 Chapter 28: revise

The chapter previously asked what civilization had been “selecting for” across ten millennia, called freedom and consent “the direction of the process itself,” and treated reduced politics as civilization's ultimate measure. That converted a chosen political criterion into naturalized historical purpose.

The revision keeps the historical observations and the chapter's program while:

- separating propagation from improvement;
- acknowledging retained, rebuilt, and renamed coercion;
- naming agency and reduced coercion as this book's chosen standard;
- making protocol gains conditional on contestability, corrigibility, and answerability;
- replacing technical *attractor* language with the specified capture incentive;
- limiting strategic claims about principled behavior to repeated interaction, partner choice, enforceable commitments, and defenses against exploitation.

### Volume 7 Chapter 32: revise

The chapter already says migration to protocol is a design hypothesis rather than a law of history and preserves roles for voice, remedy, realistic exit, and bounded authority. Its final sentence nevertheless announced coordination “evolving beyond coercion.” The new close preserves the aspiration without reinstating a guaranteed political terminus.

## Decision

Keep empirical progress, cultural selection, and political evaluation distinct. A historical trend can support a chosen program; it does not choose the program for us.
