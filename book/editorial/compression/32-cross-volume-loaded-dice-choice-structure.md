# Cross-Volume Audit: Loaded Dice, Choice, and Structure

## Scope

This audit tests the recurring coin-and-dice family for duplicated work and for an inference from aggregate distributions to individual choice:

- Volume 1 Chapter 9 uses a coin to separate physical Measure from observer Credence.
- Volume 2 uses coins and dice for entropy, Bayesian updating, Everettian self-location, small-world probability, decision theory, and idealized agreement.
- Volume 5 Chapter 20 uses dice in the air as an image for prospective risk.
- Volume 6 Chapter 23 uses loaded dice for the distribution of parenthood costs.
- Volume 7 Chapter 17 and Volume 8 Chapter 24 link that parenthood analysis without re-deriving it.

## Ownership rule

A coin or die is not one argument. Retain a worked example when the randomizer isolates a different quantity, model, or decision problem. Cut only a casual reintroduction that repeats the same calculation.

The loaded-dice analogy carries a further limit. A tilted aggregate distribution can be compatible with biological constraints, institutions, voluntary specialization, dependence, bargaining inequality, discrimination, norms, and interactions among them. The tilt alone proves neither coercion nor free consent.

## Findings

### Probability examples: retain

The quantitative uses do distinct work:

- Volume 1 Chapter 9 tracks Measure and Credence through one physical case.
- Volume 2 Chapter 9 uses binary entropy.
- Volume 2 Chapter 11 distinguishes epistemic probability from physical or statistical support and then performs a Bayesian update.
- Volume 2 Chapter 14 contrasts collapse with Everettian self-location.
- Volume 2 Chapter 22 contrasts earned small-world compression with an underspecified `p(doom)`.
- Volume 2 Chapter 23 uses the St. Petersburg game and Newcomb reliability for separate decision-theory problems.

The remaining mentions are compact contrasts, not repeated calculations.

### Volume 6 Chapter 23: revise

The chapter opens with a multicausal guard and later says that household choice may contain income dependence, constrained childcare, employer inflexibility, cultural pressure, poor information, or bargaining inequality. Its Layer 3 recap nevertheless called the aggregate distribution “assembled entirely from voluntary throws, shaped by efficiency rather than ideology.”

That sentence inferred voluntariness from the distribution and contradicted the chapter's own consent guard. The revision retains the loaded-dice image while naming heterogeneous options, bargaining positions, norms, and institutions. The observed tilt establishes neither discrimination nor free consent without separate evidence.

### Later links: retain

Volume 7 Chapter 17 invokes loaded parenthood dice only to identify unchosen starting conditions. Volume 8 Chapter 24 links the chapter as one contributor to aggregate pay gaps and explicitly says it is not exhaustive. Neither recap repeats the causal model or overstates its verdict.

## Decision

Keep the distinct probability examples and the loaded-dice motif. Do not let an aggregate distribution adjudicate the voluntariness or wrongfulness of the choices composing it.
