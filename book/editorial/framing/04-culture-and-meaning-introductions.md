# Phase 8 Framing Audit: Culture and Meaning Introductions

## Scope

This audit compares the Volume 8–9 introductions with the reviewed chapters they frame. The cluster must keep cultural selection descriptive and mechanism-bearing, deny automatic access to hidden motives, distinguish analogy from shared literal structure, and present naturalistic reconstruction and meta-myth as offered and chosen.

## Volume 8

The introduction already preserves the edited volume's controlling limits:

- pattern identity requires a stated grain and comparison;
- selection requires variants, transmission or retention, differential outcomes, environment, and horizon;
- people remain the choosing and responsible agents;
- groups and institutions qualify as agents only by independently meeting the agency criteria;
- egregores, curved landscapes, and pathogens remain controlled metaphors;
- capture uses one criterion across factions;
- asymmetry prompts investigation of omitted variables rather than proving motive; and
- propagation can decouple from accuracy without making truth irrelevant by definition.

No manuscript change is needed.

## Volume 9

The introduction already distinguishes semantic meaning, existential meaning, sacredness, and spirituality; allows meaning to be inherited, encountered, revised, and made; crosses visibly from argument into first-person commitment; marks future-mind polytheism as speculative; treats the Law of Coherence as a chosen orientation rather than a discovered law; makes provenance one resource rather than a binding inheritance; and calls the naturalistic reconstruction offered rather than the secret content of every tradition.

Two phrases nevertheless exceeded those boundaries:

- “three maps of the same terrain” implied one literal referent where Chapter 14 explicitly compares analogical treatments of structure and flux; and
- “the one life the machinery grants him” turned a naturalistic explanatory framework into a giver and narrowed an agent-general claim to a gendered recipient.

The revision names three analogical maps and asks how a finite agent lives within finite conditions. It leaves the disclosed commitment, meta-myth, and double envoi intact.

## Decision

The final two introductions now promise the edited cultural and existential arguments without converting selection into agency, asymmetry into motive, analogy into identity, or nature into an assigning subject. No title, part structure, cross-link, meta-myth status, or final-letter language changes.
