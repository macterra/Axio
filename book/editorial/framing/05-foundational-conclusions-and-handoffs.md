# Phase 8 Framing Audit: Foundational Conclusions and Handoffs

## Scope

This audit reads the closing chapters of Volumes 1–3 as both conclusions and outbound promises. It tests whether an ending restores certainty removed from the body, derives a later normative claim from an earlier descriptive one, or assigns work to the next volume that the next volume does not perform.

## Volume 1

“Why There Is Something” already closes at the correct register. It:

- offers a conditional dissolution rather than a causal origin story;
- distinguishes Everettian event realization from a stronger realization principle across law models;
- denies that a nonempty possibility space guarantees concrete realization;
- leaves realization, privileged laws and measures, consciousness, and normativity as open bridges; and
- states that concrete realization remains a premise at the edge of the account.

No manuscript change is needed.

## Volume 2

“The Beginning of Wisdom” correctly closes the epistemic volume around constraint, frame honesty, disciplined updating, criticism, and transmission under epistemic integrity. Three formulations nevertheless crossed into later volumes:

- interaction with other agents was said to place justificatory boundaries by itself;
- normative constraints were said to arise merely once agency was taken seriously; and
- truth, legitimacy, and agency were all said to begin at the concluding epistemic discipline.

The first two formulations derived a normative limit without naming a standard. The revision separates causal interaction from justification and states this book's chosen commitment to agency as the normative premise. The final cadence now makes epistemic responsibility the beginning established here and says that agency, value, and legitimacy must build on it without being settled by it. This also avoids the internal contradiction of saying agency begins only after a volume that opened with a bounded agent.

## Volume 3

“Passing the Torch” already leaves succession unresolved, treats artificial successors as possible rather than inevitable, requires independent evidence of agency, and makes preservation values explicit rather than automatic.

One sentence nevertheless said coercion and harm are evil precisely because they destroy authorship. That made harm imply wrongfulness, made both categories necessarily destructive, and presented a chosen ethical verdict as if it had been established since Volume 1. The revision hands the question to the later ethics: it asks when coercion or harm wrongfully overrides or damages authorship.

## Decision

The foundational closes now hand off physical open bridges to epistemology, epistemic discipline to later normative work, and machine classification to ethics without claiming that an earlier volume has already settled the next one's question. No chapter title, heading, unresolved verdict, optional physical model, or succession tradeoff changes.
