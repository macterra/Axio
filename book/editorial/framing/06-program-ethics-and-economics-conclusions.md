# Phase 8 Framing Audit: Program, Ethics, and Economics Conclusions

## Scope

This audit reads the closing chapters of Volumes 4–6 as conclusions and outbound promises. It tests whether the alignment program's coda exceeds its dated evidence, the ethics close collapses harm into wrongfulness or classification into justification, and the economics close asks prices to settle standing or authority.

## Volume 4

“The Program” already closes at the correct register. It:

- narrates the manifesto's discarded claims rather than silently retaining them;
- separates agreement and coherence theater from external constraint;
- records the failed growth hypothesis and the lapsed Measure agenda;
- preserves the February 23, 2026 cutoff;
- repeats the construction record's security and federation non-claims; and
- scopes sovereignty as an engineering property to the implemented, tested, single-sovereign trusted-host substrate.

No manuscript change is needed.

## Volume 5

“The Price of Agency” correctly frames its final claim as a design tension under this framework's chosen conception of agency, denies that every preventive institution destroys freedom, and limits the impossibility claim to guarantees against every intentional harm. Five intermediate formulations nevertheless restored stronger claims:

- everything making an act evil was said to precede the harmful act, although the chapter defines evil as intention plus material harm;
- any preemptive option reduction was called harm outright without a material setback, baseline, or separate wrongfulness test;
- every eradication system was forced into an exhaustive two-path dichotomy and said to have historically administered evil;
- cultural sanctions were guaranteed to stay local even though coordinated reputational, employment, housing, or membership controls can scale; and
- harm or credible threat automatically justified defensive coercion, collapsing classification into authority and proportionality.

The revision restores the sequence from formation and intention to material setback, applies the prospective-harm baseline and wrongfulness conditions, presents the eradication paths as recurrent risks rather than an exhaustive theorem, makes locality depend on pluralism and realistic exit, and says defensive coercion can be justified only under evidence, authority, necessity, proportionality, minimally harmful means, and review.

The final price remains unchanged: under this book's chosen valuation, robust authorship preserves some possibility of misuse, while eliminating every such possibility would require exhaustive control of relevant choice.

## Volume 6

“Coordination Is Not Salvation” already preserves the relevant boundary. It distinguishes price signals from moral or political authority, applies symmetric causal burdens to markets and states, keeps prosperity multicausal, denies that any mechanism supplies a universal constitution, and hands consent, harm baselines, standing, and coercive authority to the ethical and political volumes.

No manuscript change is needed.

## Decision

The middle-volume closes now preserve the program's evidence ceiling, the ethics' chosen premise and definitional firewalls, and economics' inability to infer legitimate authority from coordination. No chapter title, heading, research cutoff, final chosen-price claim, or outbound volume ownership changes.
