# Phase 8 Framing Audit: Foundational Volume Introductions

## Scope

This audit compares the Volume 1 and Volume 2 introductions with the edited chapters they frame. The two volumes are a reciprocal foundational pair: Volume 1 supplies the optional physical branching model; Volume 2 supplies the epistemic discipline governing how strongly that model and its metaphysical extensions may be claimed.

## Volume 1

The introduction already preserved the principal register change:

- Parts I–III develop the physical-agency and branching proposals.
- Parts IV–V move into explicit metaphysical conjecture.
- the bridge among control, work, and policy-conditioned Measure remains underived.
- the Quantum Metagame and Modal Realization Principle remain optional or conditional.

Four phrases nevertheless preserved pre-edit terminology:

- the proposal “transforms” every downstream question;
- choice “filters” futures;
- identity is “a pattern with a Measure”; and
- creativity is evolution “run virtually in the skull.”

The revision says the proposal *reframes* downstream questions, makes choice a policy-conditioned causal difference without branch generation, tracks identity across weighted event sectors, and describes creativity through variation and selection within cognition. This matches the Phase 7 boundaries: Measure belongs to specified event sectors, agents do not select or create worlds, and evolutionary language requires a supplied selection mechanism.

The closing status is also corrected from draft to author review.

## Volume 2

The introduction correctly:

- defines Conditionalism reflexively and within ordinary practices;
- preserves Part V as an optional QBU module;
- separates Measure from Credence;
- allows rejection of the Everettian module without rejection of the general epistemology; and
- keeps probability pluralist and knowledge fallibilist.

One sentence said twentieth-century formal results had arrived at Conditionalism's verdict from inside logic. The body is more careful: incompleteness, non-classical logics, and formal semantics constrain particular foundational or absolutist ambitions but do not uniquely prove Conditionalism. The introduction now states that boundary.

## Decision

The foundational introductions now promise the edited argument at the correct register. No title, part structure, cross-link, or canonical ownership change is needed.
