# Phase 8 Framing Audit: Economics and Governance Introductions

## Scope

This audit compares the Volume 6–7 introductions with the reviewed chapters they frame. The cluster must preserve causal symmetry between market and state action, keep empirical progress evaluative and multicausal, distinguish political classification from legitimacy, and leave national defense and protocol governance as open design problems.

## Volume 6

The introduction already preserves the edited volume's main boundaries:

- value remains agent-relative and an unwelcome outcome is not coercion by definition;
- poverty's decline is not credited to one ideological label;
- market and state action face the same causal burden;
- property, monetary policy, and regulatory capture are mechanisms rather than an exhaustive theory;
- protocol and organism metaphors carry explicit limits;
- progress uses stated metrics, while the nuclear counterfactual remains model-dependent;
- fertility and parenthood are multicausal institutional problems; and
- prices cannot decide standing or legitimate authority.

The final cross-volume handoff nevertheless said markets “imply” their governance. That conflicts with the coda's own boundary. Markets leave governance questions open; they do not deduce their legitimate answer. The handoff now says so, and the stale “in review” footer now matches the manuscript's author-review state.

## Volume 7

The introduction correctly preserves the canonical harm and coercion definitions, separates classification from justification and authority, names the Grey Zone as controlling, bounds the taxation/slavery comparison to one dimension, marks operational incitement as a third category rather than words becoming violence by themselves, leaves defense open, and denies that the fictional coda supplies empirical evidence.

Three compressed claims nevertheless exceeded the edited chapters:

- “only individuals concretely exist as valuing agents” turned the volume's chosen moral priority into a categorical ontology, where the defining chapter claims direct evidence for persons and denies only that group labels establish a unified subject by themselves;
- “civilization is the long conversion” restored a direction of history where Chapter 28 presents reduced coercion as this book's chosen standard and an active project that can reverse; and
- protocol governance was said to have out-competed the state, while Leviathan was “not an agent at all.” Chapters 32–33 instead present a design hypothesis and define Leviathan by collapsed attributable institutional authorship even though the mechanism can continue to act, optimize, and enforce.

The revision restores the chosen premise, reversible project, proposed protocol alternatives, and precise Leviathan diagnosis. It also makes the admissible region this volume's governing political question rather than the only political question without qualification.

## Decision

The two introductions now promise the edited causal, normative, and institutional argument without turning a market outcome into authority, a political commitment into history's direction, or an institutional failure into the disappearance of every underlying agent. No title, part structure, cross-link, canonical definition, or open-case verdict changes.
